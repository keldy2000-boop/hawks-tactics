If you remember nothing else

The ten rules

Each one has a clip below. Tap a rule to jump to it. Coaches will shout the short version in training and on match day. Every example on this page is against a team playing 3-4-1: three centre backs, two wingers, two centre mids and a striker.

Part 1

Win it high

Our attacks start without the ball. Win it in their half and we’re already close to goal. Then receive it so you can play forward straight away.

Rule 1

Curve your press

NORun straight at her. She passes round you.
YESCurve your run. Block the pass to her teammate.

Curve your press

Run straight and she just passes to the player beside her. Curve it so your body sits between her and that pass. Now she has to go long or give it away. Against their back three: when the middle CB has it, curve your run to cut off the pass out to one of their wide CBs, and show her towards the other side, where our midfield is waiting.

Who: our striker starts it, with the nearest winger squeezing up.the 9 starts it on their middle centre back, forcing it wide and blocking the pass back. The 10 stays on her side and presses the wide centre back. They don't cross. The midfield squeezes up behind so the long ball is ours too.

Rule 2

Open your body

NOSquare on, back to goal. You get caught.
YESCheck your shoulder. Open up. Face forward.

Scan first, open your body

Look over your shoulder before the ball arrives, so you know where the defender is. Open your body and take it on your back foot. One touch and you’re facing the whole pitch. Against their 3-4-1, the player closing you down is usually one of their two centre mids. Know which one before the ball arrives.

Who: all our midfielders, and CBs receiving from our keeper.

Part 2

Movement

The player on the ball can only play forward if someone has moved. Six ways to make the next pass possible.

Rule 3

Pass, then move

NOPass and stand still. Your teammate is stuck.
YESPass and run. Two options, one defender.

Pass, then move

Stand and watch and the defender steps in front of your teammate. Move straight away and the defender has to choose between you. She can’t cover both. Their 3-4-1 has only two centre mids, so a quick pass and move in the middle leaves one of them chasing two of us.

Who: everyone, especially the midfield making triangles.

Rule 4

Pass to space, not feet

NOBall to her feet. The defender steps in.
YESBall into space. She runs onto it.

Space, not feet

When a teammate is running, play it into the space in front of her. Ball and runner arrive together, and the defender has to turn and chase. Against their 3-4-1, look for the gaps either side of their middle CB, and the space behind their wingers when they push up.

Who: CBs and centre mids. See the run first, then pick the space.

Rule 5

Get on the blind side

NOStand where she can see you.
YESHide behind her shoulder. She looks away, go.

Hide, then go

You’re not too slow to score, you’re too easy to see. Start behind the defender’s shoulder. The moment she turns her head to watch the ball, go. Against their back three, hide on the blind side of a wide CB: she’s watching the ball out wide and can’t see you. Thomas Müller called himself a “space interpreter” and set a Bundesliga assist record doing this.

Who: our strikerthe 9 and the 10, and any midfielder arriving late in the box.

Rule 6

Start deep, arrive late

NOStart level with her. Offside.
YESStart deep. Go when the passer looks up.

Start deep, arrive late

Hang back, then go when the passer lifts her head. You hit the line at full speed while the defender is still turning, and you’re onside. Their back three is the line to beat: watch the CB nearest you, not their keeper.

Who: our striker and wingersthe 9, the 10 and the wing backs. Watch the passer’s head, not the ball.

Rule 7

Overlap on the outside

NOStay behind the winger. She’s stuck.
YESSprint round the outside. They can’t stop both.

She cuts in, you go outside

The clip shows a full-back. In our 3-4-1 the run comes from the nearest centre mid. A wide CB only goes if the other two CBs are covering. Winger: when she overlaps, cut inside and play her in or use the space.The clip shows a full-back. In our 2-4-2 that’s the wing back. When the 10 or the 9 drifts wide and cuts in, the wing back sprints round the outside, and the nearest CM covers the space she leaves. Against their 3-4-1, it’s their winger who has to track the overlap. She can’t mark two of us, so one of us is free.

Who: centre mids and wingers working together.wing backs with the 10 and the 9.

Rule 8

Switch the play

NOPlay into the crowd. You get squeezed.
YESSwitch it. Our far-side wingerwing back gets space.

Busy one side? Play the other

When one side is packed, their back three and their winger on that side have slid across, so their far-side winger is the only one left to cover a whole half of the pitch. Switch it long and our far-side wingerwing back has space to run into. You only see it if you’ve scanned (Rule 2).

Who: far-side wingerwing back stays wide and ready. Centre mids scan both sides.

Part 3

Finish it

All that work needs a clean strike at the end. And the same idea from the other side: how our keeper makes the goal smaller.

Rule 9

Get over the ball

NOLean back. It flies over the bar.
YESPlant beside it. Chest over the ball.

Chest over the ball

Three steps: plant your foot beside the ball, not behind it. Knee and chest over the ball. Laces through the middle and land on your kicking foot. Frank Lampard scored 177 Premier League goals from midfield like this.

Who: everyone.

Rule 10 · Goalkeeper

Close the angle

NOStay on your line. Half the goal is open.
YESStep out about 5 metres. The goal looks tiny.

Close the distance, close the angle

Your reach doesn’t change, but stepping out makes it cover far more of the goal from the striker’s view. Outfielders, flip it: if their keeper rushes out, shoot early or go round. Their 3-4-1 has one striker, so a one-on-one usually comes from a ball over the top. Read it early and step out.

Who: our keeper.
