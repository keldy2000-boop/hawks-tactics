Leadership

Our leadership team

Instead of one captain, we have a leadership team of three players at any one time. Players join and step off the team during the season, because we want every girl to have the chance to play a part in a leadership role.

Game day captain: for each match, one of the three is picked as captain.

What we expect from the leadership team

  • Lead by example: on time, in full kit, and ready to work at training and on match day.
  • Help lead the warm-up and get everyone switched on before kick-off.
  • Encourage teammates, especially when things aren't going our way.
  • Speak to the referee and the other team with respect, and shake hands at the end, win or lose.
  • Share ideas with the coaches about how the team is getting on.

How we coach

Our approach: process over outcome

We want to win, and the girls are competitive. That's a good thing. But we can't fully control the score, so it isn't the only way we judge a game.

Plenty of things matter, and a team can lose a game and still do all of these brilliantly:

EffortAttitudeDecision-makingLooking after the ballEncouraging teammatesEnergyInvolvementEnjoyment

So we focus on the process. Nobody can just decide to win, but we can control the things that make winning, and playing well, more likely. Get the process right and good results tend to follow.

How the girls can affect the outcome

Every player controls these, and they're where you can really help at home.

Energy & involvementLook after yourself before the game

A good night's sleep and the right food and drink mean more energy to press, make runs and stay involved. It starts the night before, not at kick-off.

TechniquePractise, don't just hope

A better first touch, pass or finish comes from practising and using the feedback we give, not from hoping it turns up on match day. Ten minutes in the garden adds up.

Decision-makingListen and watch football

Better decisions come from listening, asking questions and watching good players in your position. Notice why they do things, then try it yourself.

AttitudeHandle the frustration

Things won't always go their way. Being disappointed without sulking or giving up, and getting straight onto the next job, keeps them and the team in the game.

None of these depend on being the most talented player. Every one of the girls can choose them, and they're habits that help well beyond football.

How you can back this at home: help with the process (good sleep and food before games, a few minutes' practice, watching football together), and praise effort, attitude and good decisions, not just goals and results.

Get behind them

Sideline support

Home or away, we'd love plenty of support from the sidelines. Positive encouragement gives the girls the freedom to try things, make their own decisions and enjoy their football. Get behind them and enjoy the season with us.
